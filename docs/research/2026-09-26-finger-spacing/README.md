# Finger-key spacing — research, 2026-09-26

> **Historical record** (CLAUDE.md §6): what was found on this date, with its
> sources. Not corrected later. The layout it fed is `layout.lh_gaps` /
> `rh_gaps` in `config/body.yaml`.

**Question:** the 11 top keys run in one straight line (LH 5 upper, RH 6
lower), played with the finger pads. The provisional pitch was 24 mm; the
owner says they need to be closer. What do real instruments and hands say?

**Answer:** pad-played instruments put adjacent holes **~20–23 mm** apart
(soprano recorder), adults play down to **16–17 mm** (sopranino), and finger
pad widths put touching fingers **~15–22 mm** apart centre to centre (P5 woman
to P95 man). 24 mm is at the wide end; **19–21 mm** within a hand is the
evidence-backed target, tighter toward ring/little, with the widest gap
between the hands.

13.5 … 20.2. **Relaxed, curved fingertip spacing is unmeasured** in anything
found (Garrett AMRL-TR-69-42 may have it; DTIC 403).

## Makers' guidance

- Yamaha: holes are "not at regular intervals… the space between holes covered by the middle finger and the ring finger is also minimized" [15].
- Von Huene ergonomic recorders add keys to reduce the first-to-last spread and the right index–middle spacing [16].
- The gap between the hands is usually the largest; the right hand's little-finger hole is usually the tightest, and makers offset little/ring holes sideways rather than stretch them — which a single straight line cannot do, so there the pitch must shrink instead.
